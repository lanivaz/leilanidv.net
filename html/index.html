About Me

I spent the summer after my freshman year in London, pricing hurricane risk inside the Lloyd's market. Yokahu is a team of under ten building parametric weather insurance, and an approved Lloyd's Coverholder and Lloyd's Lab alumnus. My job was to find a way to price a margin that adjusts to each insured location, instead of charging the same flat number everywhere.

What surprised me most was how much of the work came down to judgment. Deciding which assumptions were worth defending, and how to explain a tail risk calculation to people who weren't going to open the spreadsheet.

Earlier in my freshman year I worked on a Goldenson Center case competition about how CVS Health and Aetna could address social determinants of health in underserved communities. Both projects came back to the same question: how do you get coverage to people the market usually skips? That's the work I want to keep doing.

I've also run Lovely by Leilani, a sea glass jewelry business, since middle school. Six years of setting prices and guessing how much inventory to bring to a craft fair turns out to be decent preparation for pricing work. I still make every piece by hand.

I'm looking for a Summer 2027 actuarial internship, open to any practice area.

Yokahu

Jun 2026 – Jul 2026 · 2 mos · Internship

London, England, United Kingdom · Hybrid

  • Replaced a flat margin applied uniformly to every insured location with a dynamic one priced off each location's own contribution to portfolio catastrophe risk.
  • Built the pricing model in Excel, using Python and generative AI, drawing on 41,000 simulated years of hurricane losses from the Reask DeepCyc event set to measure portfolio tail risk and how much individual locations compound or offset each other's exposure.
  • Priced each location off its marginal contribution to the portfolio's worst 1% of years (Tail Value at Risk). Concentrated locations got loaded, geographically diversified ones got a credit.
